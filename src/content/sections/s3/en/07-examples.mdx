It is not just for small projects. AWS itself publishes how companies use S3, and each example below links to the source.

<CardGrid>
  <Card
    title="Photobox"
    source="https://aws.amazon.com/s3/customers/"
    sourceLabel="Amazon S3 customers"
  >
    The photo book and printing service moved **10 petabytes** of photos from its own servers to S3.
    That is 10 million GB.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Ryanair"
    source="https://aws.amazon.com/s3/customers/"
    sourceLabel="Amazon S3 customers"
  >
    The airline replaced tape backups with S3 Glacier and saved **65%** on backup costs.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Zalando"
    source="https://aws.amazon.com/s3/customers/"
    sourceLabel="Amazon S3 customers"
  >
    The online fashion store keeps its analytics data in S3 and saves **37%** a year with S3
    Intelligent-Tiering.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Nasdaq"
    source="https://aws.amazon.com/s3/customers/"
    sourceLabel="Amazon S3 customers"
  >
    The stock exchange stores critical data in S3 and S3 Glacier.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

Notice how different these uses are: photos, backups and data analysis. It is the same service, with the same pieces you just learned.
