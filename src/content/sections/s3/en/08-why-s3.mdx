It is a fair question. An external drive is cheap and you buy it only once. The difference shows up when something goes wrong or when usage grows.

| Situation       | Your own drive or server                    | Amazon S3                                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| The disk breaks | You lose whatever had no copy               | The data is already copied across at least three zones    |
| Usage grows     | You buy another disk and move everything    | Space grows on its own, with no limit on the total stored |
| How you pay     | For the whole disk, full or empty           | Only for the GB stored, month by month                    |
| Outside access  | You set up the server, network and security | Internet access built in, controlled by permissions       |

A designed durability of 99.999999999% sounds like just a big number. Doing the math: with 10 million objects stored, the expected loss is one object every 10,000 years, on average.
