It can, and not just versions. A <Term id="lifecycle-rule" /> changes the class of objects or deletes them on its own, based on each object's age.

Think of a website's access logs. They are useful in the first month, checked now and then afterwards and forgotten by the end of the year.

Move time forward and watch the file drop down the classes.

<S3Island name="lifecycleAnimation" />

Two things to watch. By default, objects smaller than 128 KB do not change class, because the move fee would cost more than the savings.

And some classes charge a minimum storage time. Deleting or moving before that does not give the money back.

<SmallWin>Done, you now know how to let S3 tidy up on its own.</SmallWin>
